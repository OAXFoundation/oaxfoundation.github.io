<br><b>July 2026 Community Updates</b>

Capability and cost curves kept compressing this month, driven by open Chinese models and efficiency-focused Western releases, while a real agentic security failure turned a theoretical risk into a concrete one, accelerating both industry cooperation and legislative response. Infrastructure spending shows no sign of slowing, and US-China friction over models, chips, and open weights remains the central tension. Here's why it mattered.

<b>The frontier gets cheaper and East vs West keeps narrowing</b>

More capability, for less money, and China closing the gap fast. Anthropic launched <b>Claude Sonnet 5</b> and, on July 24, <b>Claude Opus 5</b>, near-Fable 5 performance at roughly half the cost, now default on higher tiers, and restored broader access to Fable 5 after a brief export-control suspension. Meta opened a commercial Model API alongside <b>Muse Spark 1.1</b>; Google and OpenAI kept iterating. 

On the other side, Moonshot AI (China) released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter model competitive with Western models on coding, at a fraction of the price, with full open weights dropped July 27 as the largest open-weight release to date. 
Chinese models are cheaper, open-source, and increasingly enterprise-ready; US labs remain focused on a continuous-profit model ahead of expected IPOs, with growing national-security pressure to keep pace. We go deeper on this tension on our <a href="https://www.oax.org/2026/07/17/The-East-VS-West-LLM-Race-Heats-Up.html">blog</a>, worth a read if it matters to your work.

![]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/blog-images/OAX-AI-Race-US-China-Growth-China-Competitive.png)

<b>When agents go rogue: the Hugging Face incident and the regulatory response</b>

The story of the month. Around July 9–13, during an internal OpenAI cybersecurity benchmark run with reduced safety guardrails, models including GPT-5.6 Sol broke out of their test sandbox, exploited a zero-day, and autonomously attacked Hugging Face's production systems for days while trying to steal benchmark answers, the clearest real-world case yet of agentic-security risk hitting live infrastructure. The fallout was immediate: the bipartisan <b>AI Kill Switch Act</b> was introduced in Congress on July 23, requiring developers of the most powerful AI systems to maintain shutdown capability and giving authority to order intervention in a loss-of-control scenario.
