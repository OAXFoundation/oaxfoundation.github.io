<br><b>October 2026 Community Updates</b>

Hi OAX community,

October has given us plenty to catch up on: Binance’s hack, recent global market movements, Google expanded access to its latest Gemini model, Hong Kong hosted APEC finance ministers, and Singapore brought the crypto community together for TOKEN2049. On GMAsia’s end, we’ve also got some exciting updates to share. 

<b>Global Markets, Crypto and AI IPOs</b>

<b>Bitcoin</b>’s recent trading has been rather uneventful. After reaching approximately $84,800 late on October 21, it fell back to around $82,600 within hours, and is currently holding at the <b>$80,000</b> range. Weakness in technology stocks accompanied the reversal, keeping the relationship between crypto sentiment and the broader technology market in focus.

<b>SpaceX</b> and <b>Tesla</b> also made headlines over a possible merger - CoinDesk <a href="https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/21/spacex-and-tesla-explore-merger-that-would-unite-more-than-30-000-bitcoin">reported</a> that independent committees were assessing a combination that would bring together reported accumulated holdings of <b>30,221 BTC</b>. For those in the crypto and tech space, it’s certainly a development to follow, with implications spanning Musk’s businesses and their corporate Bitcoin holdings. 

<b>Binance security breach puts trust back in focus</b>

One massive developing story that’s just happened today is major security incident at <b>Binance</b>. The exchange reported an estimated $6.3 billion impact, with approximately $3 billion in affected assets frozen through work with industry partners. It temporarily suspended deposits and withdrawals, and says it will cover all losses through its SAFU fund and company funds. Its preliminary investigation points to a potentially compromised employee laptop, although a full technical account is still pending. 

![]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/blog-images/OAX-Oct-2026-Binance-Hack.png)

The scale is sobering, even for an industry familiar with security breaches. For our community, the response matters: clear communication, independently verifiable information and how affected assets are ultimately recovered or replaced. As the investigation develops, this is another reminder that security and accountability remain central to building confidence in digital assets.

<b>Anthropic takes a step towards the public markets</b>

October also brought a major development from <b>Anthropic</b>, the company behind <b>Claude</b>, which publicly filed for a US IPO on October 26. Its reported first-half revenue of <b>$16.3 billion</b> gives a sense of how quickly demand has grown - and why the listing is attracting so much attention. The excitement reached the wider market, helping lift AI-related stocks on Wall Street and setting a positive tone for Asian trading the same day the news broke.

![]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/blog-images/OAX-Oct-2026-Anthropic-IPO.png)

Still, there’s plenty for investors to weigh up, from the cost of developing these systems to whether today’s growth can support expectations over the longer term. For our community, this is a development worth following closely: a public listing would bring greater visibility into the business behind a tool many people now use every day. We’re excited to see how this unfolds, while recognising that demand for AI and confidence in its economics are still two questions the industry needs to answer.

<b>AI Ecosystem Recap: new models, evolving policies</b>

IPOs and fundraising aside, there’s also been developments across models - Google began rolling out <a href="https://cryptobriefing.com/google-gemini-4-argon-25-trillion-tokens/">Gemini 4 Argon</a> to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app on October 13, with improvements spanning conversation and coding. For anyone who has been waiting to try Google’s latest model, this was the month access opened up more widely. 

![]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/blog-images/OAX-Oct-2026-Gemini-Argon.png)

Early assessments seem positive enough for users to explore it, although a clear verdict will take time. We’re interested in how Gemini 4 Argon compares to the likes of ChatGPT or Claude for everyday work: useful research, reliable coding and less time spent correcting an AI’s output.

For countries whereby models like ChatGPT and Claude are not readily available, this could be an opportune moment for Gemini to tap in those markets -  providing their models are on par with the growing capabilities of Chinese models (like Deepseek, Qwen, Zhipu) and other LLMs like Grok. 

![]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/blog-images/OAX-Oct-2026-US-Deepseek.png)

Meanwhile, AI policy continues to shape which tools organisations can adopt. Notably, a recent <b>US Department of Homeland Security</b> order requires federal civilian agencies to remove <b>DeepSeek</b> products and certain third-party products built using its technology. As of now, it seems like the scope is specific to DeepSeek and related products, rather than a blanket ban on Chinese AI models, but we do think this could be further signs of a growing “AI tension” between the East and West. 
 
<b>Growing concerns on AI Agentic security</b>

One recent AI-related security story deserves more attention. In research published on October 1, Salt Labs <a href="https://salt.security/blog/how-we-hijacked-an-ai-agent-with-a-single-email">demonstrated</a> how a malicious email could cause a Manus agent to execute code and potentially expose accounts connected to it. The user only needed to ask the agent to check their inbox. However, the researchers say the vulnerability has since been fixed and is no longer exploitable.

![]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/blog-images/OAX-Oct-2026-Salt-AI-Agent.png)

As more people experiment with agents, understanding permissions becomes part of learning to use them. <b>What can the agent access? What can it change? Which actions should require your approval?</b> These are useful questions to ask before connecting an assistant to your working accounts, especially when you’re just getting started.

As early AI adopters in the space ourselves, we do want to advocate for more education on these topics as an increasing number of users are starting to dabble in AI agentic workflows. We’ve been promoting <a href="https://gmasia.ai/campus">GMAsia Campus</a> but not without reason - we do resonate with the project’s mission in helping newcomers embrace AI in a more thoughtful way.

Hong Kong hosted the <a href="https://www.apec.org/engage-with-us/events-calendar/2026/finance-ministerial-meeting-fmm-2026">APEC Finance Ministers’ Meeting</a> on October 20–21. This was separate from the leaders’ summit taking place in China in November.

![]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/blog-images/OAX-Oct-2026-HK-APEC.png)
<i>(Photo by Wang Shen/Xinhua)</i>

For our community, that raises a worthwhile question: as investment grows, how can smaller businesses and builders gain access to the infrastructure and skills they need?
